Detect Law v8.1

A zero-shot AI-text detector: two small open models and an exact integer decision rule, with no training on AI text. These specs match the version scored by RAID's official evaluation.

How a text is checked

  1. Normalise. Unicode NFKC, remove invisible format characters (zero-width spaces, soft hyphens), map Cyrillic and Greek look-alike letters to Latin, collapse spacing.
  2. Clean (app only). Skip headings, table fragments and equation lead-ins, and everything from a References heading on. PDFs are reflowed into paragraphs first.
  3. Window. Split into 200-token windows, each scored from a fresh start-of-text token; a final window under 100 tokens joins the one before. The RAID run read the first 1,024 tokens of each text.
  4. Measure every token, with one forward pass of each model:
    • Surprise: how predictable the token is under model A, against that position's own expected value.
    • Contrast: how much more the instruct model B prefers the token than the base model A does, against the expected gap.
    • Repetition: whether tokens repeat within the window, against how much probability A already gives tokens it has seen.
  5. Decide. An integer sequential test over the three signals stops early when the evidence is decisive, or finishes with an end-of-text test. Its human baseline allows for differences between texts. Every constant comes from 3,200 human RAID training texts (400 per domain). The rule keeps 24 int32 words of state and uses no floating point.

Known limits

  • Texts under about 120 words are often Undecided.
  • Paraphrased text and base models with plain random sampling are the hardest cases.
  • RAID's AI models date from 2023. Short texts from current frontier models are harder to catch.
  • Scanned PDFs (page images only) contain no text to read.
  • The constants were set on RAID's human training texts.
  • Not yet tested on non-native English writers.

A verdict is statistical evidence, not proof. Send AI verdicts to a person for review, never to automatic penalties.
