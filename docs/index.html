FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is Detect Law really free?

Yes. It runs on your own computer, so there is no server cost to pass on: no account, no credit card, no word limit and no trial period.

Is my text private?

Yes. Every check runs locally, and nothing you check is uploaded or stored anywhere else. After the one-time download it works offline.

How accurate is it?

On RAID's official test of 672,000 texts it caught 92.33% of AI-written text while flagging 5% of human text, 88.99% including adversarial attacks, and 86.54% at a strict 1% false-positive rate. That is the highest score of any zero-shot detector on RAID. Trained commercial detectors score higher on RAID; Detect Law needs no training on AI text and costs nothing.

Which AI models can it detect?

On RAID it was tested on text from ChatGPT, GPT-4, GPT-3, GPT-2, Llama chat, Mistral, MPT and Cohere models, catching 82% to 100% depending on the model. Text from chat models (Llama 2 chat, ChatGPT, Mistral chat) was caught 99.6–100% of the time. Newer frontier models are harder to detect in short texts; longer documents give much stronger evidence.

What do the three verdicts mean?

AI evidence: the text shows statistical evidence of AI writing. No evidence: an AI-sized effect was ruled out. Undecided: no significant evidence either way, which is where most human writing ends up.

Can a verdict prove someone used AI?

No. A verdict is statistical evidence, not proof, and a small share of human texts are flagged (0.22% in our tests, more for poetry). Use it to decide what a person should read closely, never to penalise anyone automatically. Human writing heavily polished by an AI tool can also be flagged.

Does it work on paraphrased text?

Partly. Paraphrasing is the hardest attack: Detect Law caught 76.6% of paraphrased AI text on RAID, and paraphrased text is often marked Undecided.

Which languages does it support?

English.

How long should a text be?

200 words or more works best. Very short texts are often Undecided, and longer documents give stronger evidence.

What do I need to run it?

An Apple silicon Mac, or a Windows or Linux computer with Python 3.10+ and a C compiler. An NVIDIA GPU makes it faster but is optional. The first run downloads about 5 GB.
